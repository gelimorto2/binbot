# BP‑04 — Electronics & Wiring (v0.2)

Part of the [BinBot wireframe](00-wireframe.md). **Rev C: the bought base keeps
its own battery, motor controllers, brakes and remote, untouched.** This covers only
the top module's electronics and its three connections to the base. Covers power distribution,
the safety circuit, control and communications, the electronics box (E‑box),
fuses, wires and connectors. The sensors themselves (LiDAR, GNSS, cameras)
are detailed in BP‑05; here they are wired in.

## 3. How it works

- **Power (E‑01):** a fused tap (F1 40 A) at the base battery → key isolator →
  aux supplies (computer, sensors, radio) and, through **lift contactor K1**, the
  lift. Aux stays on after an E‑stop so the robot can report.
- **Driving:** the MCU commands the base's own controllers by CAN or RS485 if
  they have it; otherwise it imitates the RC receiver (PWM/SBUS) through
  changeover relay K9. The handheld remote always wins (manual mode).
- **Safety (E‑02):** 4 E‑stops, wireless E‑stop, both safety edges and the software
  watchdog are wired in **two channels** into a safety relay. Any one of them opens
  the base's **E‑stop/enable input** (base motors stop, its spring brakes clamp)
  and K1 (no lift).
  Restart needs the blue RESET button. A welded contactor blocks the reset.
- **Control (E‑03):** the computer plans and drives; the MCU does real‑time
  I/O (base drive commands, lift, weighing, display, LoRa pendant, watchdog).
  If the computer hangs, the MCU stops the robot. If the MCU hangs, its watchdog
  relay K4 opens the safety chain.

## 4. Power budget

| Rail | Loads | Typical | Peak | Supply |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base drive | motors, controllers (vendor) | — | — | base battery, unchanged |
| 24 V LIFT | 2 actuators | 0 | 480 W, ~20 s per swap | U3 600 W |
| 24 V CTRL | PC1 15, LiDAR 7, cameras 6, GNSS 2, MCU+relays 10, display 5, switch 4 | 50 W | 80 W | U1 240 W |
| 12 V COMMS | router 6, LoRa 1, beacon 8, buzzer 2 | 10 W | 17 W | U2 60 W |

A full day ≈ 0.5–0.7 kWh from the base battery, small next to the drive.
**DC/DC input range must match the base battery** (36–75 V for a 48 V base;
40–160 V types for 60/72 V).

Wire: tinned, flexible, 105 °C (e.g. H07RN‑F / automotive FLRY). Every wire
numbered at both ends. Power cables tied every 200 mm, protected where they pass steel.

## 8. Tests before first drive

| # | Test | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Insulation of our module: tap + and − to the top frame, isolator open | > 1 MΩ |
| E2 | Drive interface: MCU commands slow forward / reverse / turn, base on stands | Matches the remote; remote overrides |
| E3 | Each E‑stop, the wireless E‑stop, each safety edge, watchdog: trigger **one at a time** while the tracks run on stands | Base stops, its brakes clamp, K1 drops, reset needed |
| E4 | Wireless E‑stop out of range / battery removed | Robot stops within 0.5 s |
| E5 | Bridge K1 aux (simulated weld), try reset | Reset refused |
| E6 | Unplug PC1 USB while driving on stands | MCU stops the motors |
| E7 | Base charger plugged in (if the base reports it) | Drive refused |
| E8 | 2 h run in the sun, E‑box closed | Inside < 50 °C |

**Have the safety circuit (E‑02) and the risk assessment checked by a qualified
person (ISO 13849) before anyone works next to the robot.**
